## First public game dumper

- **OunceCake** is public under **GPLv2**:
  <https://github.com/misshexa66/OunceCake>. It was announced as the first
  public Switch 2 game dumper on 2026-10-26.
- It dumps **unencrypted romFS and exeFS** for games, updates, and DLC. The
  README lists digital games, game cards, and Switch 2 Editions.
- It is **read-only**. The author reports that system titles and applets are
  not supported, and it does not dump save data.
- It does **not** provide keys or offline decryption. The console mounts and
  decrypts the content normally, then the tool exposes the files over FTP.
- Requirements: a jailbroken console, a compatible `ns2jb` fork with
  persistent RPC, and an FTP client. The documented flow is `node run.cjs
  --rpc`, then `node ouncecake.cjs <path-to-ns2jb-run>`, then FTP to port
  `2121`.
- The initial release briefly depended on an RPC change that was not yet in
  the public fork. The public `kurumuz/ns2jb` fork added persistent RPC, and a
  later OunceCake update fixed a lockup that required rerunning the exploit.
- Internal storage and game cards were confirmed working at release. SD-card
  content was still untested.

This is **game asset access**, not system-level access. It does not supply
system NPDM metadata, system ROMFS, or an NCA4 offline decryptor.

## WebKit

- The WebKit port of the userland exploit was still **private/unreleased** as
  of this note.
- Testers reportedly used it on 23.0.1 and expected it to work on lower
  firmware. Treat this as unverified until a public release and write-up exist.

## Firmware and browser gating

- **23.0.1 remained current**; no 23.0.2 or newer update was observed in this
  window.
- Community reports describe a browser/update **"supernag"**: web applets can
  become unavailable with "This feature is not available" or error
  `2811-1007`. A pending system update keeps them blocked until the console is
  updated.
- 90DNS does not appear to prevent the nag. The check interval and exact
  mechanism are unconfirmed; community guesses ranged from roughly 12 to 24
  hours. `NIM` `RequestCheckSafeSystemVersion` was suggested as a related
  mechanism.
- Because the planned WebKit entry point needs the browser, this could lock
  users out unless a bypass is found. No bypass was confirmed in this window.

## Exploit reliability and risks

- The `ns2jb` userland exploit remains **random and unreliable**. Stage **2/4**
  is still the usual failure point.
- Anecdotal improvements include Ethernet, docked mode, and waiting about 30
  seconds after boot. Community guesses for success rates were roughly 5–10%
  for the original exploit and up to about 20% with community fixes; these
  numbers are not comparable across consoles, layouts, or forks.
- Community reports say the heap grooming can overwrite more than the intended
  FS process permissions. One tester reported losing a SmileBASIC save; another
  warned that a critical overwrite could brick the console. Treat this as a
  real stability risk, not a normal accepted failure.
- No confirmed bans from `ns2jb` were reported in this window. There is still
  no public kernel or bootrom exploit, no CFW, and no emuNAND.
- OunceCake can lock up the console on a crash, requiring the exploit to be run
  again. The first update addressed one such crash.
- **Do not write to NAND** and do not let an LLM drive NAND operations.

## Companion game-file tooling

The first game dumps led to quick updates in community tooling:

- `KillzXGaming/Switch-Toolbox` reached a final release; texture swizzling for
  Switch 2 assets is still being worked on.
- `dt-12345/nso-tools` and community hactool forks are adding ZBIC support.
- Some Switch 2 exeFS files still hit LZ4 decompression failures in existing
  loaders. Treat early compatibility reports as incomplete.
